Satellite to siren:
the whole pipeline, explained

ISRO MOSDAC hotspots + Open-Meteo weather stream through Kafka into an ML detector, tiered alerts land in Elasticsearch, and a batch MLOps backend keeps the model honest. This is what runs on your machine right now.

Two generations live in this repo — know which is which

Deployed (containers, running now): the India-focused ISRO MOSDAC + Open-Meteo streaming pipeline — its code is baked into the Docker image.

This checkout: the original gridMET / western-US generation — its code, its README, and (importantly) the trained model artifacts both generations share.

1Where the data comes from

1.1 ISRO MOSDAC — the satellite fire signal

MOSDAC is ISRO's satellite data portal. The pipeline consumes INSAT-3DR/3DS Level-2P Active Fire Hotspots (KML files — fire pixel coordinates + observation times) and Level-1C radiance rasters (HDF5) to resolve physical temperatures. mdapi.py is the token-authenticated downloader for MOSDAC's download_api (datasets set in config.json). From the raw channels it derives 7 radiometric features:

FeatureWhat it is
latitude longitudefire pixel location (4 km INSAT pixel)
t_mirMid-Wave Infrared brightness temp (K) — the fire channel
t_tir1Thermal Infrared brightness temp (K) — background channel
delta_tt_mir − t_tir1 — the core thermal-divergence fire signal
frp_mwFire Radiative Power (MW) via Wooster/Kaufman: 4.34e-19 × 16 km² × (T_MIR⁸ − T_TIR1⁸)
solar_elevsolar elevation — rejects sun-glint false alarms (hot ground at high sun)

1.2 Open-Meteo — the weather context

A free weather API (api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast) provides co-temporal conditions at each fire's lat/lon, cached on disk (mosdac_weather_cache.json) so replays don't re-hit the network. 8 weather features:

temp_crhwind_speedwind_dirprecip_mmsoil_moisturevpdfwi (computed)

The pipeline computes its own Fire Weather Index (0–100):

dryness          = max(0, 100 − rh) × 0.35     (0–35)
wind_effect      = min(wind_speed × 3.5, 30)   (0–30)
vpd_effect       = min(vpd × 12, 30)           (0–30)
soil_suppression = max(0, soil_moisture × 50)  (0–25)
FWI = clamp(dryness + wind_effect + vpd_effect − soil_suppression, 0, 100)

1.3 gridMET — the labeled training history

gridMET (Univ. of Idaho): 4 km gridded daily surface weather for the western US, joined with a historical wildfire label — the only source with ground truth (Wildfire: Yes/No). Files: Data/Wildfire_Dataset.csv and the 212,500-row eda/data/eda_sample.csv. 17 features per cell-day:

latitudelongitudeprrmaxrminsphsradtmmntmmxvsbifm100fm1000ercetrpetvpd

2How the sources combine

  • One spatial key: every record — satellite event or weather update — is keyed by Uber H3 hexagon (resolution 6, ~3.2 km edge ≈ the 4 km INSAT pixel; regions at res 3, ~60 km).
  • Stream–Table join: weather lands in the compacted wildfire.weather topic (a materialized KTable); satellite events flow through wildfire.raw. Both keyed and co-partitioned by H3 cell → streams_app does a shuffle-free left join: each fire observation is enriched with its cell's latest weather.
  • Training vs serving: the model learned from labeled gridMET history; at serving time it scores INSAT+MOSDAC events (caveat in §3.4).

3The model, features, target, metrics

3.1 What model

RandomForestClassifier (scikit-learn, 150 trees), trained by train_stream_model.py. Artifacts: artifacts/best_RandomForest.pkl (model), preprocessing_artifacts.pkl (scaler + imputation medians + feature list), stream_model_meta.json (metrics).

3.2 Training attributes & target

  • Target attribute: Wildfire — binary (Yes/No → 1/0): did a fire occur at this grid cell on this day.
  • Inputs: the 17 gridMET features above (climate, fuel moisture, energy release, vapor deficit, wind, location).
  • Split: 175,000 train / 37,500 test rows.

3.4 Honesty note — what actually scores events today

The deployed detector loads the gridMET forest (17 feature names), but MOSDAC events carry 15 different features. Only latitude/longitude overlap; the rest are filled with training medians at scoring time. So the RF probability acts mostly as a spatial prior, while the physical tier rules (§6) and a logistic fallback risk scorer (z = −4.5 + 0.38·ΔT + 0.04·FWI + 0.05·max(0, T_MIR−300)) do the real detection. No trained risk model exists yet — the detector logs Trained artifacts not found for prefix=risk and falls back automatically. Retraining on MOSDAC features (detector_model.pkl / risk_model.pkl) is the intended fix; the loader picks them up when present.

4Kafka — producers, topics, partitions, jobs

Single-node KRaft broker (no ZooKeeper), apache/kafka:3.9.0 (localhost:9092 host / kafka:29092 internal). Topic auto-create is off; all six topics are declared by create_topics.py.

4.1 Producer (one, idempotent, three modes)

ModeWhat it streams
replay (running now)the compiled MOSDAC CSV (9,356 rows) at 50 eps on a 60× synthetic clock
mosdacraw MOSDAC KML ingestion, resolving physical temperatures + co-temporal weather
livesynthetic feed anchored to 10 Indian forest reserves, same FRP/FWI math as training

Per event it writes two records: observation → wildfire.raw, cell weather → wildfire.weather. Idempotence (enable.idempotence=true, acks=all, lz4) means retries never duplicate.

Co-partitioning raw and weather on the same H3 key with equal partition counts is exactly what makes the join shuffle-free.

6How alerts are triggered

Model probability alone never raises an alert — it must clear physical tier rules combining radiometry and weather. Sun-glint correction: under zenith sun (solar_elev > 55°), ΔT must be 1.5 K stronger.

EMERGENCY — high-intensity or fast-spreading fire

p ≥ 0.80 AND ( ΔT_eff ≥ 11.2 K & wind ≥ 2.8 m/s · OR FRP ≥ 162 MW · OR T_MIR ≥ 315.5 K · OR ΔT_eff ≥ 10 & surge ≥ 2.5 & FWI ≥ 32 )

WARNING — confirmed contextual fire anomaly

p ≥ 0.65 & ΔT_eff ≥ 9 & T_MIR ≥ 309 · OR p ≥ 0.75 & FRP ≥ 125 · OR p ≥ 0.70 & rolling-max ΔT ≥ 9.5 & FWI ≥ 30 · OR p ≥ 0.65 & night & ΔT ≥ 7.5

WATCH — developing anomaly / elevated fire-weather risk

p ≥ 0.42 & ( ΔT_eff ≥ 5.8 OR FWI ≥ 25 ) · OR ΔT_eff ≥ 7.5 & T_MIR ≥ 303 & FWI ≥ 22

Each alert carries a deterministic alert_id, tier, H3 cell + region, lat/lon, a GeoJSON polygon of the cell boundary, probabilities, model names, radiometry + weather blocks, and a readable message. Dedup: max one alert per (cell, tier) per 60 s. Fan-out: Elasticsearch, dashboard SSE, notifier (WARNING/EMERGENCY dispatch).

7Why Elasticsearch

  • Geo search — location as geo_point, cell geometry as geo_shape: "every alert inside this polygon / near this coordinate" is a native query.
  • Searchable archive — full-text + structured search over 90 days of alerts (/api/search?q=emergency on the dashboard proxies ES).
  • Kibana-ready — --profile kibana brings up :5601 for ops dashboards.
  • Idempotent upserts by alert_id — at-least-once indexing can never duplicate.
  • Separation of concerns — Kafka is the streaming backbone; ES is the query tier.
